# Hermes Telegram Talk

An English Telegram Mini App for a single authenticated voice conversation. The dialogue fills the screen above a compact persistent bottom row: the three-dot settings button on the left, a larger profile avatar in the center and the microphone control on the right. The app has no heading of its own. Telegram's outer container chrome stays outside the page.

Connect, Retry saving, Retry and Enable audio remain discoverable when needed. Blocked output shows one short status line, "Audio and microphone paused.", and the Enable audio button; no error line repeats it. In Retro 95 the line moves into a window (see Notices). A launch without a chat link shows no note; the server still binds nothing without a valid `/talk` handle.

The transcript shows interim text, final corrections, Interrupted and Cut off labels as before. It keeps at most 100 lines and scrolls through the remaining height. It follows new text while the reader is at the end and preserves older reading positions on updates and rotation. It is display only. Final turns retain their existing persistence path.

## Notices

One notice sits between the status line and the control row. `render()` derives it as `{kind, title, message, action}` from the call phase, the conversation's save state and the error text, and stores nothing of its own. Its button runs `start()`, which takes the same branch in the same state. Blocked audio offers Enable audio. A failed save during a call offers Retry saving, which sends the same pending events again. A stopped call offers Retry after an error and Connect without one. An error during a live call with nothing to retry, such as a refused voice, shows its message without a button. A failed save always shows "Conversation could not be saved. Tap Retry saving." beside its button, also after Enable audio cleared the error text.

A notice with error text is an error, titled "Error". Blocked audio and a stopped call without an error are prompts, titled "Information". A prompt's message is the status line's own text from the status table, "Audio and microphone paused." or "Call stopped.", so the notice adds no second wording.

In Retro 95 every notice is a Windows 95 message window: a 1 px #222 frame with the `--raised` bevel and a 4 px black offset shadow, the Call settings title bar (black, white text, inset white highlight), an inline SVG symbol, the message in body ink and the real action. An error shows the error symbol (a #9D264B circle with a white cross and a black offset shadow). A prompt shows the classic information symbol (a white speech balloon with a blue "i" and a black offset shadow). Portrait puts the button below the message; short landscape puts it at the end of the message row. The action is a Windows 95 default button: the grey `--raised` face with ink text inside a heavy dark outline (the 1 px #171B18 border plus a 1 px #171B18 ring outside it), pressed to `--inset`. Pink appears only as its keyboard focus ring. Reconnect in Call settings uses the same default button, because it is that window's action.

While a prompt window shows the status text, the status line steps out of sight with a clipped 1 px box and keeps its text. So the message appears once on screen, and `role="status"` still says the true state. The window's copy of that text (`#notice-message`) is `aria-hidden`, so a prompt is announced once, by the status line. An error is announced once, by `#error` with `role="alert"`, and the status line above its window stays visible because it says something else.

The window has no OK and no close control. Its only button is the action, so a failed save stays visible until saving succeeds. It is not modal: it has no overlay and no focus trap, and it sits in the flow above the control row. The transcript shrinks instead of being covered, and the Call settings panel still opens over it from the control row. Render rewrites the notice text only when it changes, so the per-frame render cannot repeat the announcement. Modern draws no window: `#notice` is `display: contents`, the title, symbols and `#notice-message` are not rendered, and the status line stays visible, so the red line and the orange button render exactly as before.

An error raised while Call settings is open, such as a refused voice, sits in its window under the open panel until the panel closes; the alert still announces it. This holds in both themes and predates the windows.

## Call settings

The three-dot button opens one Call settings panel: Reply voice, Theme and End call. It is anchored above the bottom control row, so status lines, errors and the Enable audio or Retry button above the row never push it out of the viewport. It stays fully inside 390x844, 360x640, 320x568, 844x390 and 1280x800 and scrolls inside itself on a shorter screen. Short landscape lays the sections out in two columns.

Its heading has a 44 px Close button. The labeled full-width voice selector and its one-line availability note, "Availability varies by account.", form one section; a pending change adds its explanation and Reconnect there, because a live call keeps its voice until it reconnects. The selector stays enabled while a choice is saved, so the panel keeps focus and stays open. The Theme section is a radio group, Modern or Retro 95. A divider separates the full-width End call action, a self-contained block that can be removed without touching the rest.

Opening moves focus into the panel. Escape, Close and the toggle close it and return focus to the toggle; a pointer outside dismisses it. The panel follows the toggle in document order, so Tab from its last control moves on (to the microphone when it is enabled) and closes it. There is no focus trap.

## Themes

Both themes are ported from Full Duplex (`client/style.css`, `client/theme.ts`): its look, not its layout. The choice is `data-theme` on `<html>`, stored in `localStorage` under `talk-theme`. Reading or writing storage is wrapped in try/catch, so blocked storage still applies the choice to the page, and an unknown stored value falls back to Modern. `<meta name="theme-color">` follows the theme. `app.js` applies the stored theme as its first statement, before `DOMContentLoaded`. The server CSP (`script-src 'self' https://telegram.org`) allows no inline script, so a Retro 95 user can still see one Modern frame before `app.js` runs on a slow load; removing it would need a backend change (a CSP hash or one more served file).

- **Modern** is the default: Full Duplex's plain warm dark. Page #30302e, surface #262624, sunken #141413, text #FAF9F5, secondary text #C2C0B6, neutral #9C9A92, borders rgb(222 220 209 / 15-40%), accent #D97757, danger #EE8884. User turns sit in surface bubbles; assistant turns are plain text. The muted microphone is #602A28 with a crossed-out #EE8884 icon.
- **Retro 95** is Full Duplex's Typesafe, for people who like something unusual: a light sky gradient, #C9CAC4 bevelled controls (`--raised` and `--inset` box shadows), square corners, black offset shadows, a black title bar on the panel, pink #EF65AD focus and #9D264B danger. It has no ring: the portrait fills a square with the buttons' raised frame, and two slim level meters in sunken black wells stand directly left (assistant) and right (microphone) of it (see Level meters). Both bevels are drawn outside the boxes, so they never cover the image or the segments.

Shapes follow one rule per theme. In Modern, icon controls (menu, microphone, Close) are circles, and text actions (Enable audio, Retry, Reconnect, End call), the selector and the theme options are 12 px rounded rectangles at least 44 px tall. In Retro 95 every control is a square bevel. In Modern the avatar portrait stays round inside the round ring. In Retro 95 the image, or the neutral face, fills the square edge to edge with `object-fit: cover`: no circular mask, no inset and no stretching. Body text contrast is at least 4.5:1 in both themes; `tests/browser/talk-ui.mjs` measures every visible text element against its rendered background, including each stop of the Retro 95 gradient.

## Composition and decisions

The calm dark direction came from the Stripe.dev quiet technical dossier: neutral material, restrained light type and fine circular line art. Its editorial grid and decorative console are omitted. This is a working mobile screen. The Full Duplex themes now supply the palette.

Blue identifies measured microphone input and orange identifies measured assistant output in both themes and for both profiles: #4FC3F7 and #FFB74D on the Modern ring, and Full Duplex's Typesafe segment colors, #2F7DFF and #FF9A1F with lighter top segments and peak caps, on the Retro 95 meters. Silence and unusable media states are neutral. The ring uses local and incoming Web Audio RMS measurements. Silence leaves a thin circle; energy changes its width and radius. There is no random or time-driven voice effect. Nothing is drawn over the portrait: a partial arc marks connecting and a broken circle marks muted silence. Mute shows on the microphone button instead, as a crossed-out microphone on top of its pressed colors, beside the "Microphone muted." status text. Actual assistant output remains orange while the outgoing microphone is muted. Accessible labels identify each state. Reduced motion keeps fixed geometry while the measured audio still selects listening/speaking color and label.

The status line and the orb's accessible label come from one table that maps the raw ring state to a label and a minimum hold, driven by a single timer. Listening is the main state: connected silence also reads "Listening.". A change between Listening and Speaking shows only after the new state has held for 1000 ms, so short pauses change nothing. "Connecting. Microphone paused.", "Audio and microphone paused.", "Microphone muted." and "Call stopped." show at once. While the microphone is muted the text stays "Microphone muted." even when the assistant speaks. The ring canvas and its `data-state` colors stay real-time.

The font is the device's system sans serif. No font download, third-party avatar or tracking request is introduced. An original inline SVG face is the fallback. The same-origin local fallback loads first. Authenticated same-origin `api/avatar` image bytes replace it through a short-lived blob URL. Touch targets are at least 44 px and keyboard focus has a visible 3 px outline. Portrait and landscape keep the same bottom row. The 52 px controls flank a 96 px avatar ring, reduced to 72 px in short landscape. The Retro 95 square is 84 px (62 px) inside the same slot, so the row height does not change, and its 8 px meters fit in the 120 px center column. The display name stays on one line with an ellipsis, so the row height and the panel's maximum height are fixed.

Avatar load and error handlers apply to both the fallback and the authenticated blob image, whatever the deployment base path is (the default profile is served at `/tg-voice-michael/`). Only the current base's same-origin `avatar` fallback is accepted, without query, fragment or credentials. Starting a replacement call clears the previous image before bootstrap. A decode failure shows the neutral face; a temporary authenticated refresh failure keeps the last good image. No authentication, server avatar policy or image source is broadened.

### Level meters

In Retro 95 two vertical meters replace the ring. The left one, orange, shows the assistant playback the user hears; the right one, blue, shows the microphone the call sends. They port its metering (`client/levels.ts`, `client/VoiceLevels.svelte`): the measured RMS passes a 0.008 noise gate, a fixed gain of 10 and a 0.6 curve. Each call holds `{level, peak, hold}` per source in `meters`. Every frame of the existing animation loop advances both by one `follow()` step: instant rise, a fall of 0.07 per frame, and a peak held for 8 frames that then falls 0.06 a frame. Segments are 6 px tall with 2 px gaps and fill from the bottom; the held peak shows as a pale cap.

The human meter reads only an enabled, unmuted microphone. The assistant meter reads only playback through a running audio graph on a connected peer, with the element neither muted nor at zero volume. So mute empties the human meter, and a disconnect, blocked audio or ended playback empties both, each at the normal fall. Stopping the call empties both at once, and a new call starts empty. The two sources never share a value, so both fill when both speak. Reduced motion shows the plain level, still, four times a second, with no fall and no cap. The meters are `aria-hidden`; the status text and the orb's label already announce the state. Modern draws no meters. `tests/browser/run.mjs` also switches a live WebRTC call to Retro 95 and checks that real microphone and incoming energy fill the meters.

## Ownership and deployment boundary

`static/` remains the original voice test and its exact legacy verification. `static/talk/` is the v2 static bundle. The integrated server serves `static/talk/` at `/tg-voice/` whenever a Talk config is given and `static/` otherwise; `tests/browser/fixture.py` and the HTTP contract rely on that selection with no override. The frontend slice did not change the backend, stock Telegram, profiles or deployed assets.

The call resource object owns its peer, media tracks, data channel, AudioContext, analyser nodes, timer and animation callback. A replaced call cannot act on a new call. The authenticated conversation owns a bounded in-memory queue of settled text, sequence numbers and retry IDs. Reconnect revalidates bootstrap and retains events only for the same signed identity and conversation. Auth or ownership refusal discards queued events. No browser storage or audio recording is used. Failed saving exposes Retry saving after three bounded retries, with identical IDs and no new voice session.

## Protocol and readiness

Inspected primary source is Conduit `GPTLiveProtocol.swift`, `GPTLiveSession.swift` and `GPTLiveConversationController.swift` at revision `3a4a2ce461f7667e28ca75770ae10c8f166a4680` in https://github.com/kaishi00/hermes-conduit. Conduit-derived behavior remains under `LICENSE`.

Subscription events use `session.started`/`session.updated`, `input_transcript.added`, `output_transcript.added`, and final `turn.done {turn:{role,transcript}}`. Only final user/assistant turns enter event ingestion. Fragment events, public API events, reasoning and tool output do not. Any other client delegation goes to the authenticated `api/task` once per ID, whatever bootstrap said about tasks, as in Conduit `GPTLiveDelegationBridge.swift` at `4b6889097d53ad731089f255b03afe534ef3da45`. The server decides admission. Its reply or refusal is appended to that delegation through `delegation.context.append`. The client is transport, not a second task authority. Two nonvisual Pulse paths change no screen element. An assistant `turn.done` that follows user speech (`input_transcript.added`) during its output is sent with `interrupted: true`, so its text is not counted as telling an update. A `pulse P<n> ...` delegation goes to `api/pulse/feedback` once per ID, with its reply appended on `speakable` to that delegation.

Mic tracks are disabled until peer connection, open data channel, subscription handshake, running AudioContext and successful output playback. When greeting is enabled, a single supported `session.context.append` in the speakable channel requests the configured opening. Final assistant text plus quiet measured output completes the opening. This follows the inspected subscription protocol. No shared interface file was changed. The frozen bootstrap does not advertise the configured event text cap. This client uses the backend default of 2000 characters and preserves longer text in Unicode-safe chunks. Integration must retain that cap or expose the configured value before using a lower cap.

Autoplay denial or suspended audio keeps the mic disabled and exposes Enable audio. The browser may require that tap. A finite readiness deadline makes an absent handshake or greeting recoverable. Mute affects the actual outgoing track and survives retry, reconnect and temporary connection interruption within the page.

## Exit and screen lock

`visibilitychange` and Telegram minimized state do not end a call. A restored back-forward-cache document rebinds controls and initializes a fresh call. A real `pagehide` closes tracks, peer, channel, audio graph and animation, then checkpoints best effort using authenticated keepalive requests. Native Telegram closure and background termination do not reliably deliver unload events. Server expiry remains necessary. A physical iPhone, real screen lock and provider call have not been tested by this worker.
